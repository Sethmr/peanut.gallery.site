How to install.

If you can install an app from a store, you can install Peanut Gallery. One tap in the Chrome Web Store, one pin to your toolbar, one click on any YouTube tab. You’re listening inside a minute. No account, no credit card, no developer skills.

Add to Chrome.

One tap. The Chrome Web Store does the rest. When it’s done, you’ll see a little peanut icon near the top-right of your Chrome window.

Add to Chrome →
Free to try  ·  No signup  ·  Open source  ·  Works in Chrome, Edge, Brave, Arc

What you need.

§ 01 · Before you start
A browser
Chrome

Chrome is the simplest path. Edge, Brave, and Arc all work too — they’re built on the same engine as Chrome. Firefox and Safari are on the way.

An account
None

You don’t sign up. You don’t enter an email. You don’t pick a password. Install and press Start — that’s the whole setup.

A credit card
No

Everyone gets 15 free minutes of listening on our servers, no card needed. After that you can keep going for free with your own keys, or subscribe if you’d rather not juggle them.

Three steps from zero to listening.

§ 02 · The install
1

Click the big red “Add to Chrome” button

It opens the Chrome Web Store page for Peanut Gallery. You’ll see another Add to Chrome button there — click that one too. Chrome shows a little window asking you to confirm. Click Add extension.

A few seconds later, you’ll see a message: “Peanut Gallery has been added to Chrome.” You’re installed.

2

Pin the peanut to your toolbar so it’s easy to find

Look near the top-right of Chrome, just left of your profile picture. You’ll see a small puzzle-piece icon. Click it. A short list of your extensions pops open.

Find Peanut Gallery in that list. Next to it, click the little pin icon. The pin turns blue, and the peanut hops out into your toolbar so it’s always one click away.

3

Open any YouTube video and click the peanut

Go to YouTube. Open any video with audio — a podcast, an interview, a talk. Click the peanut icon in your toolbar. A tall panel slides out on the right side of your browser.

In that panel, click the big red ▶ Start Listening button. Within a few seconds, four AI critics start reacting to the video — the panel fills with their short, in-character takes in real time. You’re live.

What happens on your first run.

§ 03 · The tour

The first time you open the side panel, a short guided tour pops up. Six little cards walk you through the settings drawer — which pack of critics you’re using, how often they chime in, how to mute anyone who’s wearing you out — plus a quick note on tapping a reaction to save or share it.

The last card asks you to pick how you want to use Peanut Gallery going forward. Three buttons:

  • Try now — uses your free 15 minutes on our servers. No signup, no card. Good for deciding whether you like the thing.
  • Get my own keys — runs you through three quick provider signups, one at a time. Free forever once you’ve got the keys.
  • Peanut Gallery Plus — coming soon. $8 a month, we handle the keys for you.

You don’t have to pick right away. Skip the tour any time with the Skip button in the top-right of each card, or re-run it later from Settings → Appearance → Replay settings tour.

After the free minutes run out.

§ 04 · Keep going
Pick whichever feels like less work to you

Get my own keys

Free forever

The in-panel wizard opens three websites for you (Deepgram, Anthropic, xAI), one at a time. You create a free account on each, copy a string of letters and numbers they give you, and paste it into the box the wizard shows. About ten minutes total. After that, each provider bills you directly — roughly $1 per hour of listening, and all three include free signup credit that covers casual use for a long time.

Common questions.

§ 05 · FAQ

What’s an API key? Do I need one?

An API key is a private string of letters and numbers a service gives you when you sign up — think of it as a password the extension uses to talk to that service on your behalf. You only need keys if you pick “Get my own keys.” The in-panel wizard shows you exactly where to click on each provider’s website, and gives you a paste box for each key. You won’t be asked to write any code.

Where does my audio go? Does YouTube know I’m using this?

Your audio is sent to the AI providers so they can transcribe it and generate the reactions. We don’t save or record it — it’s streamed in real time, turned into text, and discarded. YouTube can’t detect the extension (we use Chrome’s built-in tab-audio feature, the same kind of thing meeting tools use), and the extension does nothing when no session is running. See the privacy page for the full breakdown.

I use Edge / Brave / Arc instead of Chrome. Does it work?

Yes. Those browsers are built on the same engine as Chrome, so the Chrome Web Store link above works the same way. On Edge you’ll see a prompt asking “Allow extensions from other stores?” — click allow. Firefox and Safari are on the roadmap; they work a bit differently under the hood and need a separate version.

What if the reactions stop or something looks wrong?

Two fixes cover almost everything: (1) close the side panel and open it again, or (2) refresh the YouTube tab. If a specific video isn’t being transcribed, it’s usually an ad playing — let it finish. For anything else, there’s a Feedback & bugs section inside the side panel settings with a one-click email link.

How do I uninstall if I don’t like it?

Right-click the peanut in your toolbar and pick Remove from Chrome. That’s it. All your settings are wiped off your computer. If you happen to be on Plus, cancel that first from the side panel (Settings → Backend & keys → Plus → Manage) — removing the extension alone doesn’t stop the monthly charge. Or email support@peanutgallery.live and we’ll handle it.

Will it slow my computer down?

No. When no session is running, the extension does nothing — Chrome puts it to sleep until you click the peanut. When you are listening, the transcription and reactions run on remote servers, not on your computer.
