Cover Story

Four AIs walk into your video tab.

A writers' room for whatever you're already watching — live in Chrome's side panel, reacting as the transcript comes in.
By the Gallery Staff
Filed 4:12 PM · Pressroom wire

Peanut Gallery is a free Chrome extension that opens a running commentary alongside any video or audio tab — livestreams, podcasts, webinars, Zoom calls, YouTube. A fact-checker pulls receipts. A cynical troll says what chat is thinking. A sound-effects guy drops cues. A comedy writer turns the dryest term sheet into a bit.

"Finally — a peanut gallery that heckles back."

It runs in the native side panel, not on top of the page. Under 300 milliseconds from spoken word to posted reaction. Swap packs between the Morning Crew and the TWiST staff in a click. No signup. No paywall. MIT licensed.

How it works

The transcript comes in. The staff goes off.

Deepgram Nova-3 streams the captions. Claude Haiku handles the fact-checker and comedy writer — the two roles that benefit from nuance. xAI Grok Fast powers the troll and sound-effects guy — reflexive, punchy, no deliberation. Grok Live Search pulls receipts when the fact-checker needs one.

The Rack
  • Deepgram Nova-3 — transcription
  • Claude Haiku — fact-check, comedy
  • Grok 4.1 Fast — troll, sound FX, fact-check receipts

Plus did not ship.

Plus was planned as a flat-rate hosted backend ($8/mo, 16 h/wk, no keys to juggle). It did not ship. The project was unpublished from the Chrome Web Store on 2026-05-02 before the subscription went live. There is nothing to buy here. The pricing page documents the original design for posterity.

Letters to the editor

Pg. A6 · Opinion
To the Gallery —

"Does this thing send my browsing to anyone? I watch some weird stuff, is what I'm saying."

— Concerned in Cupertino
Editor responds
Audio from the active tab goes to Deepgram for transcription; transcript snippets go to Anthropic and xAI for reactions. That is it. No history, no profiling, no ads. Self-host the backend and even that round-trip is yours to audit. The extension never touches a tab you haven't opted in on.
To the desk —

"You talk a big game about 300ms. What actually happens between the video and the side panel?"

— J. P., latency snob, Austin TX
Editor responds
Deepgram Nova-3 streams partials as the words land. Each partial fans out to the four critics in parallel — Haiku for fact & comedy, Grok Fast for troll & FX. First tokens come back in 120–180ms; the feed renders as they stream. Nothing waits for anything else.
Dear editor —

"I don't want a fact-checker. I want four Joke Writers. Can I do that?"

— Pack-curious, Brooklyn
Editor responds
Yes, and welcome to the party. A pack is a JSON file — four personas, four prompts, four model picks. Write a Joke-Writer-only pack, sideload it, name it whatever you want. The Pamphlet has the schema and a worked example.
To the staff —

"Does it work on anything besides YouTube?"

— Podcast loyalist, Vermont
Editor responds
Yes. Any Chrome tab with audio — podcast players, livestream platforms, webinar tools, even a Zoom window. The transcript hook is cleanest on captioned video today; the generic tab-audio pipe is on the bench and already permissioned. Watch the github for pre-release builds or subscribe to the Sunday bonus (see page A5, circa 2027).
Hey —

"Why Morning Crew? Why TWiST? Why not just name four roles and move on?"

— Anonymous, via the chat
Editor responds
Because a fact-checker is a boring job title and The Producer is a person. Personas bring voice, cadence, in-jokes — the things a role description can't specify. Swap them for the Lex Fridman pack, the NBA on TNT pack, the Mystery Science Theater pack. The role is the floor; the persona is the ceiling.
To the editor —

"Wait, is this free or is it $8 now? What changed?"

— Economics-curious, Oakland
Editor responds
Self-host with your own keys. Paste your own Deepgram, Anthropic, and xAI keys, run the backend yourself, never pay us a cent. The Plus tier — a planned $8/mo flat-rate hosted backend — never shipped; the project was unpublished from the Chrome Web Store before launch. The source is MIT, on GitHub; if you stand up a hosted backend yourself, you're the operator and you carry the legal exposure for what the personas say.
